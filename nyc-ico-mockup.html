For schools and youth agencies

A season of outdoor trips for your students, at no cost.

NYC ICO works with public schools and community agencies across the five boroughs. We currently partner with more than twenty, from third grade through high school. Here is what the partnership involves, in plain terms.

What NYC ICO provides

  • One or two certified trip leaders plus assistant leaders for every trip
  • Transportation, food, and gear (packs, layers, tents, sleeping bags)
  • Insurance coverage through the Sierra Club
  • Trip planning, permits, safety plan, and first aid
  • Access to 150+ trained volunteers who sign up per trip
  • Training for a staff member who wants to become a certified leader

What your school provides

  • A staff contact who comes on trips and helps with the pre-trip meeting
  • Dates cleared with your calendar, roughly every four to eight weeks
  • Your own permission process, alongside ICO's form
  • School buses when you have them (we rent vans when you do not)
  • A flier to families describing the trip, departure and return times
  • A signed Letter of Agreement and a short application, once

What a year looks like

Trips build on each other. A group that starts with a park walk in the fall is ready for an overnight by June. This is the pattern our longest-running partner, PS 241, has followed with fourth and fifth graders for sixteen years.

Fall and winter

  • October: Fall hike at Bear Mountain or Van Cortlandt Park
  • January: Ice skating in Central Park or at Bear Mountain

Spring and summer

  • March: Nature sanctuary or farm, with a lesson tied to class
  • June: Overnight camping trip, the year’s capstone

Questions schools ask

What does it cost?

Nothing. ICO covers transportation, food, gear, and permits. We raise our own funds through Sierra Club membership, donations, and events.

Who supervises the students?

Certified leaders are Sierra Club members who hold current Wilderness First Aid certification, have completed Outdoor Leadership Training, and are screened through the New York State Central Registry. Your staff member is on the trip too.

What about insurance and liability?

Trips run under Sierra Club insurance. Every student needs a signed ICO permission form, available in English and Spanish, in addition to whatever your school requires.

Three steps to a first trip

  1. Reach out

    Email nycico.partners@gmail.com. Tell us the grade level and how many students.

  2. Sign the agreement

    A New Agency Letter of Agreement and a short application. Once, not per trip.

  3. Meet your leader

    We match you with a certified leader, or train one of your staff, and pick a date for the first outing.

For certified leaders and assistants

Everything for planning and running a trip, in one place.

Four planning phases, the essential forms, and a link to the destination finder. If you are new to leading, start with the timeline and shadow two trips.

Trip timeline

6 to 8 weeks out
  • Pick a destination and clear the date with the agency
  • Get Chair approval
  • Post the trip on Meetup so volunteers can RSVP
  • Reserve the van or confirm school buses
2 to 4 weeks out
  • Scout the route and order trail maps
  • File permits or concessionaire forms
  • Write the safety plan
  • Send the flier and permission forms to the agency
Week of the trip
  • Hold the pre-trip meeting with students and staff
  • Confirm headcount, drivers, and medications
  • Plan snacks and meals and pull gear
  • Print maps, directions, and the trailhead talk
Trip day and after
  • Collect permission forms, adult waivers, and the Code of Ethics
  • Lead and sweep, take regular headcounts, and hold a closing circle
  • Thank volunteers and ask for photos
  • Log the trip in Campfire, file expenses, and return gear

Volunteer

Come on one trip. Most people come back.

You do not need outdoor experience to start. Attend one orientation, and you can join any trip on the calendar. Leaders are trained from within.

  1. Attend a New Volunteer Orientation

    Held quarterly, currently online. Email nycico.newvolunteer@gmail.com for the next date.

  2. Apply and sign the Code of Conduct

    A short application form. The Code of Conduct is signed at orientation.

  3. Join the Sierra Club and the Meetup group

    Membership covers you under Sierra Club insurance. Meetup is where every trip is posted, and access opens after orientation.

  4. Go on trips

    RSVP on Meetup. A certified leader runs the day; you help with headcounts, games, snacks, and conversation.

Becoming a certified leader

What it takes

  • Current Sierra Club membership
  • Background check (New York State Central Registry)
  • Wilderness First Aid certification (ICO offers a course each January)
  • Outdoor Leadership Training 101
  • Shadow a certified leader on two trips, then lead one provisionally
  • Sign-off from the Chair

How training works

  • New Volunteer Orientation and all-volunteer meetings are held quarterly
  • Wilderness First Aid is a two-day course, generally offered in January
  • Outdoor Leadership Training 101 is a one-morning course
  • New leaders shadow two trips before leading provisionally

About NYC ICO

Access to the outdoors is a basic right.

Inspiring Connections Outdoors is the Sierra Club's program for young people with limited access to nature. When asked what keeps them from spending time outdoors, young people in this country name nearby access and transportation first. ICO removes both, and adds trained leaders, gear, and food.

History

1970The San Francisco Bay chapter starts the first ICO group. There are now 43 across the country. 1981The New York City group is founded. 2014Inner City Outings becomes Inspiring Connections Outdoors, with the same mission and a clearer name. 2019An Equity, Inclusion and Justice Committee forms to examine ICO's own practices and partnerships. TodayMore than 300 members, about 30 adult and 40 youth certified leaders, and 20+ partner schools and agencies.

Our values

We are part of the Sierra Club's Outdoors for All campaign. Our partner groups are primarily Black, Asian-American, and Latinx, and we work to build a volunteer base that reflects the young people we serve. Leading trips is not enough on its own. We aim to run trips that contribute to larger change: partnerships with organizations led by the communities we work in, a more inclusive volunteer base, and closer collaboration with our partner schools.
